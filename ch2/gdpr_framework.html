GDPR Comprehensive Framework

Complete overview of the General Data Protection Regulation context and key provisions

Harmonization Goal
Unify data protection across EU
Create a consistent legal framework for personal data protection across all Member States
Ensure free flow of data
Enable movement of personal data across EU without barriers
Recitals 1-3
Replace Directive 95/46/EC
Modernize outdated 1995 directive to address technological advances
Not Applicable To
Law enforcement
Handled by separate directive (EU 2016/680) for criminal justice data
Recital 19
Household activities
Processing by individuals for purely personal or household purposes
Recital 18
National security, defense
Out of GDPR scope—left to Member States
Recital 16
Data Protection as a Right
Fundamental right under EU Charter Art. 8
Everyone has the right to protection of personal data concerning them
Recital 1 affirms fundamental rights
Data protection is tied to respect for private and family life
Territorial Scope
Applies to non-EU controllers
Even non-EU companies must comply if targeting or monitoring EU residents
Recitals 23–25
Legal Bases for Processing
Consent
Freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous indication
Art. 6, Recital 32, 42
Contract
Necessary to perform a contract with the data subject
Legal Obligation
Processing required to meet legal responsibilities
Vital Interest
To protect life or health, e.g., medical emergencies
Public Task
Official tasks or public interest roles by public authorities
Legitimate Interests
Processing necessary for a controller's legitimate interest unless overridden
Special Categories of Data
Sensitive data
Includes race, religion, health, sex life, political opinions, genetics, biometrics
Art. 9, Recitals 51–56
Requires explicit consent or strong justification
e.g., medical treatment, public health, legal claims
Data Subject Rights
Access, Rectification, Erasure, Restriction
Core rights to control and correct data held about individuals
Portability, Objection, Automated decision-making
Right to data transfer, to object to processing, and avoid automated decisions
Data Protection Officer
Required for large-scale or sensitive processing
Mandatory for public authorities or systematic monitoring
Art. 37, Recital 97
Ensures compliance internally
Advises, monitors compliance, and serves as contact point for authorities
Risk-Based Approach
Risk severity drives obligations
Higher risk = more stringent safeguards
Recitals 75–77
DPIAs mandatory in high-risk cases
e.g., profiling, large-scale surveillance, biometric data
Recitals 84–94
Data Breach Notification
To authority in 72h
Notify without undue delay if breach may risk rights/freedoms
Art. 33
To user if high risk
Communicate to affected individuals unless mitigated
Art. 34
Accountability & Compliance
Controllers must document, assess risks
Maintain records, assess DPIAs, and review data practices regularly
Implement privacy by design/default
Integrate data protection into systems by design and by default settings
Art. 25, Recital 78
Codes of Conduct & Certification
Encouraged to support SME compliance
Enable industry-led standards and certifications to prove compliance
Recitals 98–100
International Transfers
Adequacy Decisions
EU Commission declares a third country as providing adequate protection
Recitals 103–106
Safeguards
Use of SCCs, BCRs, or consent for transfers where adequacy doesn't exist
Recitals 108–110
Enforcement & Sanctions
Supervisory Authorities
Independent national DPAs oversee enforcement
Recitals 117–121
One-Stop-Shop
Lead authority coordinates for cross-border processing cases
Recital 124
Penalties
Fines up to €20M or 4% of global annual turnover
Recital 148
