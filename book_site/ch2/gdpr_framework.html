GDPR Comprehensive Framework

Complete overview of the General Data Protection Regulation context and key provisions

Harmonization Goal
The GDPR was introduced to unify data protection laws across EU Member States, ensuring a consistent level of protection.
It also aims to ensure the free flow of personal data within the EU internal market.
A cloud service operating in multiple EU countries doesn't need to follow different rules in each country.
Recitals 1–3
The GDPR replaces the outdated Data Protection Directive 95/46/EC, modernizing legal frameworks for the digital age.
Not Applicable To
The GDPR does not apply to activities related to law enforcement, which are covered under Directive (EU) 2016/680.
Police investigations are governed by separate rules.
Recital 19
It also excludes purely personal or household activities with no connection to a professional or commercial activity.
Saving your family vacation photos on your private laptop.
Recital 18
National security, military, and defense activities are also excluded from GDPR's scope.
Intelligence agency surveillance programs.
Recital 16
Data Protection as a Right
Personal data protection is recognized as a fundamental right under Article 8 of the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights.
Individuals have the right to know and control who processes their data and for what purposes.
Recital 1 affirms the importance of safeguarding this right for dignity and personal freedom.
Territorial Scope
The GDPR applies not only to entities within the EU but also to non-EU organizations offering goods or services to individuals in the EU.
A US-based website that sells products to customers in France must comply with GDPR.
Recitals 23–25
Legal Bases for Processing
Consent must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous to be valid.
Ticking a checkbox to agree to marketing emails.
Article 6, Recitals 32 and 42
Contractual necessity allows processing if needed to fulfill a contract.
Processing a shipping address to deliver a purchase.
Legal obligation allows processing if required by EU or Member State law.
Retaining payroll records for tax compliance.
Vital interests permit processing to protect someone's life.
Accessing medical records in an emergency.
Public task covers processing done by public authorities or for tasks in the public interest.
Public health monitoring.
Legitimate interests may justify processing where there is minimal privacy impact and a clear purpose.
Using analytics to improve a service.
Special Categories of Data
These include sensitive data such as race, health, political views, or biometric data, and require higher protection.
Processing genetic test results.
Article 9, Recitals 51–56
Processing is allowed only under strict conditions, such as explicit consent or legal necessity.
Data Subject Rights
Individuals have the right to access, correct, erase, or restrict their data, and to object or request portability.
A user can ask to delete their social media account and download their data.
They also have rights against automated decision-making, including profiling.
Data Protection Officer
Organizations engaged in large-scale or sensitive data processing must appoint a Data Protection Officer (DPO).
A hospital or social media company needs a DPO to ensure compliance.
Article 37, Recital 97
Risk-Based Approach
The GDPR tailors obligations based on the risk posed to individuals' rights and freedoms.
Using facial recognition software requires stricter controls than collecting email addresses.
Recitals 75–77
Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) are mandatory for high-risk processing operations.
A bank using AI to evaluate loan applications must conduct a DPIA.
Recitals 84–94
Data Breach Notification
Organizations must notify supervisory authorities of personal data breaches within 72 hours if there is a risk to individuals.
Notifying the data protection authority after customer records are hacked.
Article 33
If there is a high risk to individuals, they must also be informed directly.
Alerting users if their passwords are exposed in a breach.
Article 34
Accountability & Compliance
Controllers must implement data protection measures and be able to demonstrate compliance.
Documenting policies and staff training programs.
Privacy by design and by default must be built into systems and processes.
Default settings on an app must favor privacy.
Article 25, Recital 78
Codes of Conduct & Certification
Industry codes and certifications are encouraged to help especially SMEs comply.
A startup using a GDPR-certified CRM platform.
Recitals 98–100
International Transfers
Transfers to third countries can occur if the European Commission has issued an adequacy decision.
Data can be transferred to Japan, which has such a decision.
Recitals 103–106
If not, safeguards like Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs), Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs), or derogations must be used.
A multinational uses BCRs to transfer HR data between EU and US offices.
Recitals 108–110
Enforcement & Sanctions
Independent national supervisory authorities are responsible for enforcement.
France's CNIL or Ireland's DPC.
Recitals 117–121
The one-stop-shop mechanism ensures a lead authority handles cross-border complaints.
Ireland leads cases for companies like Meta.
Recital 124
Serious violations can result in fines up to €20 million or 4% of global turnover.
Amazon was fined €746 million under GDPR.
Recital 148
